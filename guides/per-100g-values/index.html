Guide

How to read per-100 g nutrition values

A per-100 g number is only as useful as the record it comes from. Dry or cooked, raw or boiled: the state of the food changes everything.

One food, several records

USDA FoodData Central keeps separate records for the same food in different states. Rolled oats on this site are measured dry: 379 kcal per 100 g. Brown rice is measured cooked: 123 kcal per 100 g. The gap is mostly water, not a difference in the grains.

Three checks before comparing

  1. State. Is the record dry, raw, cooked, boiled or drained? Compare like with like.
  2. Salt. Cooked legume records here are “without salt”. Canned or salted versions carry more sodium.
  3. Edible portion. Values cover the part you eat: egg without shell, fish without bones.

From 100 g to your plate

Multiply each value by your portion weight and divide by 100. A 40 g serving of dry oats holds 40 ÷ 100 = 0.4 times the amounts listed. The portion calculator does this for you.

Where the numbers come from

Every food page shows its FoodData Central record number in the dark source strip under the title. Follow it to see the full USDA record, including nutrients not listed here.
