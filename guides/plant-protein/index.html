    Plant protein: what 100 g really gives you

    Seeds and nuts top the per-100 g table, but most of their energy is fat. Cooked legumes give less protein per 100 g but far more per calorie.

    Protein per 100 g

    • Pumpkin seeds30.2 g
    • Peanuts25.8 g
    • Almonds21.2 g
    • Flaxseed18.3 g
    • Walnuts15.2 g
    • Rolled oats13.2 g
    • Edamame11.9 g
    • Lentils, cooked9 g
    • Chickpeas, cooked8.9 g
    • Black beans, cooked8.9 g
    • Kidney beans, cooked8.7 g
    • Split peas, cooked8.3 g
    • Whole-wheat pasta, cooked6 g
    • Quinoa, cooked4.4 g
    • Buckwheat groats, cooked3.4 g
    • Bulgur, cooked3.1 g
    • Brown rice, cooked2.7 g
    • Pearl barley, cooked2.3 g

    The catch for each group

    • Nuts and seeds are dry and fatty, so they lead per 100 g but lag per calorie. See protein per calorie.
    • Legumes are measured cooked here, so water lowers their per-100 g figure. Per calorie they beat every grain.
    • Grains are mostly starch. Oats are measured dry, which is why they look stronger than cooked grains.

    Compare two directly: lentils vs chickpeas, peanuts vs almonds, quinoa vs brown rice.
